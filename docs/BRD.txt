PCB Assembly Quality Intelligence
Business requirements for a ProcessGrit demonstration
Prepared for the ProcessGrit development team and the prospective PCB assembly customer. Build a working demo that connects approved product requirements to first-article approval, production evidence, release decisions and corrective-action effectiveness.
The central scenario is the reported U3 orientation incident involving 1,200 affected units. Demonstrate how an ambiguous requirement is identified before release, how missing inspection coverage is exposed, and how affected serial numbers are contained when a defect is discovered.
What the customer must be able to see
Business question
Visible demonstration
Can we prevent the wrong setup from entering production?
A controlled Pin 1 requirement, independent verification and a blocked first-article release.
Why did the defect escape?
A gate-by-gate coverage and evidence map separating occurrence from escape.
Which boards are exposed?
Serial and panel genealogy linked to material lots, program revisions and inspection history.
Can we act before yield deteriorates?
SPC drift warnings and a clearly labelled predictive-risk example.
Did the fix work?
Corrective-action evidence, repeat inspections and an effectiveness review across subsequent lots.
Scope and evidence boundary
Inputs reviewed: the user’s 8D review narrative and the two supplied flow images. The underlying 8D, U3 manufacturer part number, actual production records and machine interfaces were not supplied. Do not portray an escape mechanism, machine capability, numerical limit or model accuracy as a verified customer fact.
Scope is contract PCB assembly and motherboard assembly, from order/specification alignment to dispatch and feedback. Bare-board fabrication, automatic machine control and a full ERP replacement are outside the demo. All proposed controls require plant confirmation before live use.
Deliverable: a resettable application with linked screens, synthetic records, working gates, imports, calculations, reports and acceptance tests. This BRD defines the build; it does not claim that the tool or a production AI model already exists.

3  Proposed route and release logic
Proposed configurable route: specification alignment → incoming material and setup → paste print/SPI where available → SMT placement → reflow → post-reflow AOI → required X-ray → DIP/wave when applicable → depanel and cleaning → agreed electrical/functional test → final QA release → dispatch. The first article traverses its applicable route before approvals authorize batch production.

State machine and exceptions
Order states: Draft → Engineering review → Ready for first article → First article pending → Customer approval pending if required → Released for batch → In production → Final QA pending → Released for dispatch → Closed. A hold can suspend any active state; resolving it returns the record to the appropriate review stage, not directly to release.
CTQ outcomes are Pass, Fail, Unknown or Not applicable. Missing evidence is Unknown. Not applicable requires a route-based reason and approval. A concession is a separate, visible authorization with scope, expiry, quantity, approvers and compensating controls; it never rewrites a failed result as Pass. Non-waivable controls remain blocked.
Changes to the BOM, footprint/orientation, placement or inspection program, critical material, reflow profile or test plan trigger an impact review. Reverify affected CTQs and obtain fresh approvals before further release. Record what changed and which existing units are exposed.
The demo blocks actions inside the application. A physical line-stop or ERP shipping interlock requires a later validated integration; do not imply a software hold alone stops equipment.
4  Specification alignment and first article
S01  Order and engineering alignment
Pain point: Conflicting documents and experience-based interpretation can propagate the same error into placement and inspection.
CTQs and controls: Approved product/revision, BOM and MPN; component side, Pin 1 and orientation; contractual acceptance class and revision; measurable test limits; explicit coverage for every critical failure mode.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K01 CTQ definition completeness; K02 validated critical coverage; unresolved engineering conflicts count.
Import BOM and placement CSV; upload datasheet/assembly drawing and agreed test plan. Engineering manually resolves missing fields and marks the source page/section. No package means G0 is blocked.
Analysis and response: Compare revisions and orientation fields, list mismatches and assign an engineering query. Authoritative evidence is an approved product-specific manufacturing package reconciled against the exact manufacturer package drawing; silkscreen or Gerber alone does not resolve a conflict.
S02  First article verification and approval
Pain point: A golden board or two signatures can reproduce the same assumption if there is no independent reference.
CTQs and controls: Verify each critical CTQ against approved source evidence; record U3 Pin 1 photograph, side and coordinate convention, independent engineering/QA review, applicable AOI/X-ray/test results and customer approval.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K03 first-article first-pass approval; K04 approval lead time; K02 critical coverage.
Serial-linked digital checklist, reference and actual photos, machine result import, named approvals and customer email attachment. If no test SOP exists, agree one or route an explicit concession; appearance inspection does not count as functional testing.
Analysis and response: Display reference versus actual image and missing coverage. First article remains blocked until required results and distinct approvals exist. Reject stale approval evidence following a revision change.
U3 orientation record required for the demo
CTQ-U3-ORI: product revision; RefDes U3; manufacturer part and package; package drawing revision; top/bottom viewing convention; footprint Pin 1; board datum; source rotation and machine rotation mapping; expected Pin 1 location; reference image; evidence method; check frequency; applicability; engineering owner; approvers; reaction plan. Store orientation as a mapped relationship, not merely “0 degrees”.
5  Material setup and paste printing
S03  Incoming material and storage
Pain point: Wrong parts, mixed reels, moisture exposure or expired material can create intermittent defects.
CTQs and controls: MPN/BOM match, supplier/reel/PCB lot identity, material acceptance status, moisture handling where applicable, paste expiry and approved storage conditions.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K05 incoming lot rejection; K06 material genealogy completeness; expired or unidentified material holds.
ERP/store CSV plus barcode scans and supplier certificates; manual receipt form if no ERP. Moisture/paste clocks from timestamped issue/return records. If exposure history is absent, show Unknown and require disposition.
Analysis and response: Stratify later defects by supplier and material lot; do not infer supplier causation from correlation. Block rejected, unidentified or expired material pending authorized review.
S04  Line setup and program verification
Pain point: Correct parts may be placed with an incorrect feeder, side, program or rotation.
CTQs and controls: Feeder-to-BOM mapping, program/revision hash, machine coordinate convention, approved setup, required calibration and ESD verification.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K07 setup first-pass conformance; program mismatch count; K06 traceability completeness.
Machine setup export/API where supported; scan reel and feeder positions. Fallback: versioned setup checklist, program file checksum and independent verifier. Screenshot evidence alone cannot establish every placed component.
Analysis and response: Compare expected and actual setup; block start for any critical mismatch. Repeat verification after changeover, feeder reload or engineering change as defined by the control plan.
S05  Solder paste printing and SPI
Pain point: Paste variation may be discovered only after reflow. This stage is an addition to the supplied flow, subject to plant confirmation.
CTQs and controls: Approved stencil/paste, deposit volume/height/area and offset where measured; printing settings and paste exposure time.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K08 SPI first-pass yield; K09 paste CTQ out-of-spec rate; continuous trend by pad family.
SPI export with panel/board/pad IDs and numeric units. Without SPI use documented sample measurement or visual results; classify visual results as categorical and sampled. No numeric SPI means no paste capability index.
Analysis and response: Trend comparable pads and detect shifts; inspect stencil condition, paste and printing setup. Authorized process engineer approves adjustments and verifies the next article.
6  Placement and reflow control
S06  SMT placement
Pain point: Missing, wrong or rotated components can replicate across the entire batch.
CTQs and controls: Correct MPN, location and side; Pin 1/polarity; placement X/Y/rotation within approved limits; feeder and program identity.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K10 verified orientation defect PPM; placement first-pass yield using K08; mismatch count.
Placement program/setup and event export; inspection results with RefDes. If placement telemetry is unavailable, link feeder scan and program snapshot to the batch and use per-board AOI or manual verification evidence. Program commands are not proof of actual placement.
Analysis and response: Hard-block known U3 mismatch. Pareto confirmed defects by RefDes, feeder, program and material lot; inspect the station and compare physical evidence. Use only validated orientation detection coverage.
S07  Reflow soldering
Pain point: An oven setpoint may look normal while the board’s actual thermal profile is outside the approved window.
CTQs and controls: Product-specific measured peak, time above liquidus, ramp/soak where required, conveyor speed and recipe identity. Limits come from qualified materials/product requirements, not generic values.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K11 qualified profile conformance; K12 recipe/telemetry excursion rate; SPC trend on suitable continuous parameters.
Profiler CSV with thermocouple positions and profile ID; oven temperature/speed logs linked by time and route. Manual periodic profiling and recipe checks if no live export. Oven air temperature is a proxy, not a measured joint temperature.
Analysis and response: Compare profile measurements with approved limits; associate boards with recipe and time window. Flag drift for engineer review. Hold the exposure window after a critical excursion and determine the affected interval using the last verified good condition.
Example reaction rule
If CTQ-U3-ORI fails, create a critical NCR, prevent application release, identify all units using the suspect setup/program since the last verified conforming check, and request independent inspection. A good average process score or a low model risk must never override this failure.
Measurement validity
Store units, measurement method, calibration status and measurement uncertainty where applicable. Evaluate numeric measurement repeatability and inspector agreement before relying on small shifts. Orientation is categorical; do not compute Cp/Cpk for it.
7  Optical and hidden joint inspection
S08  AOI and operator adjudication
Pain point: Inspection may omit the failure mode, use an incorrect reference, or permit unjustified overrides.
CTQs and controls: Program revision and inspected feature coverage; observable Pin 1/polarity; component presence and placement; visible solder conditions; mandatory review of alarms.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K13 AOI false-call fraction; K14 audited miss rate; K15 required inspection completion; K10 orientation defect PPM.
Board/RefDes-level AOI result and images plus reviewer disposition. Fallback CSV or manual results with annotated photos. Distinguish machine calls from confirmed defects and preserve initial results. Without independent audit labels, miss rate is Unknown.
Analysis and response: Pareto confirmed defects and false calls separately; compare programs and reviewers. A validated challenge/reference set should include orientation errors where feasible. Do not assume optical inspection can certify hidden joints or metallurgical solder quality.
S09  X ray inspection where required
Pain point: Hidden-joint defects can escape surface inspection; the extent of X-ray coverage may be unclear.
CTQs and controls: Applicable BGA/QFN or other hidden-joint features; approved acceptance criteria; recipe/image quality; inspected sites and review results. X-ray is not automatically a Pin 1 control.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K15 X-ray required coverage; K16 confirmed hidden-joint defect rate; void/feature measurements where the agreed method supports them.
AXI/X-ray results and images linked to board/component/joint. If no integration, upload report and image references. If equipment is unavailable, use an approved external inspection or hold required units; a visual check is not an equivalent substitute.
Analysis and response: Compare by package, paste and profile. Report sampled coverage separately from 100% coverage. The supplied image states all hidden joints; adopt that as the demo default for applicable boards and confirm actual feasibility with QA.

8  Through hole assembly and finishing
S10  DIP insertion and wave soldering
Pain point: Polarity, missing parts and poor joints may be introduced after SMT inspection.
CTQs and controls: Correct through-hole part, orientation, seating and insertion; approved wave/selective/manual solder process; accessible joint acceptance and relevant thermal settings.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K17 through-hole first-pass yield; polarity defect count; process-setting conformance.
Insertion checklist, solder equipment export and post-solder inspection. Manual serial-linked records and photos if equipment cannot export. Product route defines applicability; no through-hole components means approved Not applicable.
Analysis and response: Add a post-DIP inspection gate. Stratify joint and polarity defects by component/process. Do not carry forward an earlier SMT pass as proof that newly inserted components conform.
S11  Depaneling and cleaning
Pain point: Mechanical stress, board damage or residues can be missed when the route records only completion.
CTQs and controls: No visible board/component damage; approved depanel method and settings; cleanliness/residue limits where required; drying and handling criteria.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K18 finishing damage rate; K19 cleanliness compliance; rework quantity.
Serial-level inspection and cleaning batch/recipe records; numeric cleanliness results where specified. Fallback sampled external/lab test and documented visual examination. Record sampling scope; visual cleanliness does not establish ionic cleanliness.
Analysis and response: Trend by panel position, equipment and cleaning batch. Hold affected boards on critical damage or failed required cleanliness results. Reinspect after corrective work with the specified method.
Panel and board genealogy
Assign each panel a parent ID and every board a child serial and panel position. Preserve this mapping after depaneling. A panel-level pass applies to a child board only when that position and its required features were included. Missing or excluded positions remain unverified.
Rework loop
Failed board → NCR and hold → authorized rework instruction → technician execution → required repeat inspection and test → independent disposition. Store every attempt and the original failure. Rework never erases first-pass failure or automatically clears related material/batch holds.
When a board is scrapped, record serial, defect, quantity basis and authorized disposition. Reconcile started units into good, held, rework, scrapped and WIP states without double counting.
9  Testing final release and customer feedback
S12  Electrical and functional testing
Pain point: A power-on pass may cover only a small part of the motherboard’s required functions.
CTQs and controls: Approved supply rails/current, boot/firmware, required interfaces, memory and specified functions; U3-related function/net where applicable; approved fixture and test software. ICT/flying probe and other tests are optional route elements, not assumed installed.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K20 functional first-pass yield; K21 retest recovery; K02 validated test coverage; K15 required test completion.
Tester CSV/API with serial, run/attempt, test-case ID, value/unit/limits, firmware, fixture and program revision. Manual instrument readings with a controlled test form if necessary. No agreed test method means a coverage gap, not a pass.
Analysis and response: Analyze failing test steps and parametric drift; separate board faults from fixture faults. Detect passing only after retries. Product engineer approves limits and test coverage, with QA controlling release.
S13  Final quality and dispatch
Pain point: Incomplete tests, unresolved holds or untraceable boards may be shipped through administrative oversight.
CTQs and controls: All required route results current, serial identity, closed required NCRs, approved deviations, quantity reconciliation, packaging/label and release evidence.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K22 release dossier completeness; K23 first-pass route yield; K06 genealogy completeness; K24 customer defect PPM.
Join production ledger, inspection/test runs, NCRs and shipment manifest. Fallback serial scan plus uploaded release checklist. If records cannot be reconciled, hold the release; do not fill missing results with estimates.
Analysis and response: Generate the shipment release dossier and explicit blockers. Customer complaints link to shipment and serial cohort; distinguish confirmed defective units from potentially exposed units.
S14  NCR and corrective action effectiveness
Pain point: Closure may record an action without demonstrating that occurrence and escape controls improved.
CTQs and controls: Separate validated occurrence and escape causes, implemented control changes, PFMEA/control-plan revision and independent effectiveness review.
KPIs
Data acquisition and fallback
K25 containment lead time; K26 CAPA effectiveness completion; K27 recurrence rate; K28 data readiness.
NCR/CAPA records plus post-action lot results and challenge checks. Manual structured workflow works without machines. Missing post-action observations keep effectiveness pending.
Analysis and response: Track recurrence, coverage and execution across the agreed validation window; reopen failed actions and horizontally assess similar components/products.

Common dimension and ownership rules
Filter by customer, product/revision, order, lot, line, machine, shift, RefDes, component/material lot and program revision when available. Engineering owns K01–K02; QA owns acceptance/yield/escape metrics; process engineering owns process CTQs; data steward owns K28. Each stage owner reviews its KPI daily or per lot, with critical alerts evaluated on event receipt.
Targets are customer-configurable. Mandatory release evidence and critical coverage use a demo target of 100%. Defect, yield and response targets require a baseline and plant agreement. Do not invent a customer target or compare unlike products without stratification.

14  Analysis and corrective recommendation engine
Analysis sequence
First validate the evidence. Then segment comparable products/revisions and plot defects, yield and coverage. Use Pareto views to prioritize failure modes and stratify by material, program, station and time. Drill from every chart to the source records. Show correlation as a hypothesis until an engineering investigation validates causation.
Analysis
Implementation and output
Rules and coverage
Compare measured results with approved limits; detect program/revision mismatches, skipped gates and missing critical evidence. These are deterministic controls.
SPC early warning
Use I-MR for suitable individual continuous values, X-bar/R for rational subgroups, and p charts for proportions with changing sample counts. Never pool unrelated CTQs or revisions.
Demo SPC rule set
Versioned rules: one point beyond 3 sigma, eight consecutive points on one side, or six monotonically increasing/decreasing. Show which rule fired and its baseline.
Capability
Only evaluate suitable numeric CTQs with approved specifications, valid measurements and a stable process. Show sample size, period and assumptions. Suppress capability for unstable, sparse or inappropriate data [R1].
Escape analysis
Map each applicable gate: expected detection, execution, capability validation, result/override and evidence. Distinguish not run, not capable, misconfigured, missed, overridden and unknown.
Recommendation
Use approved reaction plans to suggest containment and verification. Link evidence and uncertainties; assign owner and due date. Human approval is required for process changes.
SPC implementation details
For the demo, require at least 30 eligible individual baseline observations or 25 rational subgroups; mark shorter histories “baseline pending”. These are demo rules, not universal adequacy criteria. Baselines are reviewed and frozen by the process engineer; do not recalculate limits automatically after a defect.
Control limits describe baseline variation; specification limits define acceptability. Display them separately. An in-spec drift can trigger investigation, and an out-of-spec result remains a failure even if statistically expected. EWMA can be added later for smaller shifts.
Cp uses specification width divided by six times the estimated within-process standard deviation. Cpk uses the smaller distance from the mean to either specification limit divided by three times that deviation. State the estimator, assess distribution/measurement assumptions and report uncertainty; do not infer capability from pass/fail counts [R1].
15  Predictive AI requirements
Provide three visibly different outputs: rule-based release blockers, statistical early warnings, and model-based predictions. For the initial demo, use a reproducible synthetic-data model or an explicitly labelled simulated prediction. Never show a synthetic score as validated plant performance.

Start with an interpretable logistic baseline and compare a tree model only if useful. Split by time and hold out complete lots; keep all observations/retests from a board together. Features must exist before the prediction timestamp. Exclude downstream test results, final dispositions and future aggregates from upstream models.
Report held-out lot count, class balance, precision, recall, PR-AUC, calibration and false alerts per 1,000 boards at the chosen threshold. Compare against the rules-only baseline. Plant QA sets the missed-defect tolerance and alert workload; do not promise a universal accuracy target or arbitrary minimum row count.
A prediction stores model version, as-of timestamp, horizon, available features, missingness, risk band, score type and main contributors. Probability is shown only for an appropriately validated/calibrated model. Unseen products, stale inputs, drift or insufficient defect labels produce “prediction unavailable” with a reason.
Before live use, run shadow evaluation over representative lots and shifts; approve thresholds and monitoring with QA. Retrain only after review. AI suggests actions; it cannot waive a CTQ, approve a customer concession, alter machine settings or certify a root cause.
16  NCR reporting and effectiveness closure
Occurrence and escape remain separate
For U3, “orientation was interpreted incorrectly” is an occurrence hypothesis until confirmed against the approved source and setup evidence. Potential escape hypotheses include omitted AOI polarity coverage, a wrong golden reference, untested U3 function, or release without current evidence. These are demo investigation paths, not conclusions about the customer.
Report section
Required content
Problem and scope
Product/revision, failure mode, detected-at gate, evidence, confirmed defective count, potentially exposed count and shipment/WIP status.
Immediate containment
Hold scope, serial manifest, material/program/time filters, owner, start/completion times and verification. Broaden scope when genealogy is incomplete.
Occurrence investigation
Evidence-backed 5-Why chain, competing explanations, tests performed, verified causal factor and prevention action.
Escape investigation
Gate coverage/execution map, independent detection evidence, missed/overridden checks, verified escape cause and detection action.
Correction and prevention
Disposition/rework of affected units versus permanent process/control change; owner, due date, approval and implementation evidence.
System updates
Linked PFMEA/control-plan/CTQ revisions, training and program changes; horizontal applicability review across similar parts/products.
Effectiveness decision
Predefined cohort/window and criteria, actual results, recurrence and coverage, reviewer decision, residual risk and customer evidence if required.
Sample U3 correction report behavior
Generate a draft stating: a critical orientation mismatch requires containment; approved orientation evidence and detection coverage must be verified; affected units are identified by exact setup/program scope where possible. Include expected versus observed orientation and links to the original events. Every AI-written conclusion is marked Draft pending QA review.
Effectiveness plan for the demo
Illustrative plan: validate corrected orientation mapping and detection challenge cases, inspect all required U3 placements, then monitor three consecutive 200-board lots across relevant setups/shifts. Require zero confirmed U3 orientation defects, 100% required evidence and no unauthorized bypass. This 600-board example is a demo plan, not a universal closure rule or proof of zero future defects.
If no failures occur in 600 independent representative observations, the approximate one-sided 95% upper defect-rate bound is 3/600, about 0.5%; clustered board failures weaken that inference. QA must agree the actual validation exposure, independence assumptions and customer return observation period before closure.

Deterministic metric fixture
For the 200-board test cohort: 180 boards pass functional test first time; 20 initially fail; 15 recover after authorized rework/retest; 5 are scrapped. K20 = 90%, K21 = 75%, final test yield = 97.5%. Preserve the 20 original failures. If another 10 boards required upstream rework, with no overlap, all-route first-pass count is 170 and K23 = 85%.
